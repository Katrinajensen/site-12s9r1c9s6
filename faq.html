FAQ

Questions we get asked

Is this "no sugar" or "no fructose"?

Fructose-free specifically. That's a different, narrower claim than sugar-free — the label will say exactly what sweetener is used once a formulator locks the recipe.

Why is there no iron in any line?

Any supplement containing iron is legally required to ship in child-resistant packaging under the Poison Prevention Packaging Act. That conflicts with a pouch designed to open like a fruit snack, so we left iron out of every line — including Women's, where iron is a legitimate nutritional need for many people. If that's a dealbreaker for you, a separate iron supplement is worth pairing with your daily pouch.

Is this safe for kids to mistake for candy?

The Kids line is formulated without iron or B12, and dosed for children specifically — but it's still a gummy that looks and tastes like a treat, so it should be stored the way you'd store any vitamin, out of easy reach of small children.

Is this FDA approved?

Gummy vitamins are regulated as dietary supplements, not drugs, so there's no FDA pre-market approval step — the brand is responsible for safety and truthful labeling. Manufacturing will follow GMP (21 CFR Part 111), and every pouch will carry a proper Supplement Facts panel.

Why 8 different gummies instead of one multivitamin gummy?

So each nutrient can be dosed on its own rather than compressed into a single blended gummy. It's more manufacturing complexity, but it's the tradeoff we chose for a more honest label.
